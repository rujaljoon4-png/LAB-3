This repository contains my Day 3 practical laboratory work for the Generative AI for Business course as a BBA Aviation Management student. The activity focuses on understanding AI hallucination and guesswork and learning how vague prompts can cause Generative AI tools to produce assumed, invented, incorrect, or unsupported information.

The lab compares outputs from ChatGPT and Google Gemini using aviation-related scenarios, including flight-delay announcements and fictional airline compensation policies. It identifies missing information in prompts and evaluates whether AI-generated details are invented, assumed, verified, or safe for business use.

The project also demonstrates how improving a prompt with specific facts, limitations, and verification instructions can produce safer and more reliable responses. A verification placeholder such as [VERIFY WITH OFFICIAL SYSTEM] is used to prevent AI from guessing when important information is unavailable.

The portfolio includes hallucination audits, output evaluations, vague-versus-improved prompt comparisons, fictional policy testing, an original aviation hallucination test, and student reflections. The activity highlights the importance of checking AI-generated information before using it in aviation operations or passenger communication.

This work demonstrates responsible and practical use of Generative AI in aviation management, with emphasis on accuracy, verification, prompt quality, risk awareness, and human judgement.
